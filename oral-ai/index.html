AI-Driven Oral Health: a two-tier research program — oral–systemic risk prediction · a salivary gene panel for cancer screening

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR (CLINICAL & LAB LEAD)Dr. Shalini Kapoor — Research Dean. Research direction, ethics, cohort & wet-lab validation, corresponding author.
CO-PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR (COMPUTATIONAL LEAD)Kamal Preet Singh — AI/ML Engineer & Data Analyst. Data pipelines, modeling, validation, first-author manuscripts.

Program summary. A response to the Strategic Research Leadership Meeting's call — "connect ideas, create collaborations." Published dental-AI is abundant but rarely calibrated, externally validated, fairness-audited, or clinically translated — the field's own reviews document this gap. This program attacks it with two projects on one shared computational framework: Tier 1 — reverse oral–systemic risk prediction on NHANES (fast joint paper, public data only); Tier 2 — an AI-minimal salivary biomarker panel for oral cancer screening (TCGA → saliva → clinical validation in the collaborator's lab). Tier 1 builds the publication record while Tier 2 ramps; both run the same rigor spine: temporal validation, calibration, fairness audit, XAI.

1 · Why this program

Dental AI publications are growing rapidly, but 2025 reviews report poor calibration, low reproducibility and scarce external validation (PMC12286321; S0300571225003112); even implant-AI models are judged not ready for case selection (S2772559626002737), and fairness auditing remains exceptional rather than standard (JAMA Netw Open 2811316). Each project below closes those four gaps on a dataset where the question is genuinely askable — and the pair is chosen so that the first results arrive within months, on public data, before any laboratory commitment.

Each tier's novelty case — prior work, its limitations, what we do differently, and the funding angle — sits inside its own section below. One-page visual handouts are in the left sidebar.

3 · Tier 1 — Reverse oral–systemic risk prediction (fast joint paper)

Concept. Existing work predicts periodontitis from systemic factors (2019 NHANES model — our baseline); clinical prototypes screen dental patients for diabetes with simple scores (Lalla 2011; JADA 2014; DDS score validated on NHANES 2009–2014). We upgrade the proven concept to modern ML: routine dental exam features flag undiagnosed diabetes and cardiovascular risk — a triage flag ("get a blood test"), never a diagnosis.

  • Data — NHANES (free, public): ≈5,000–10,000 participants per 2-year cycle, each with BOTH a dental exam and blood results. Train 2009–2014, test 2015–2018.
  • Methods — harmonization; logistic regression / random forest / gradient boosting; temporal validation; probability calibration; subgroup fairness audit (fairlearn); SHAP → distilled chairside score.
  • Benchmark — head-to-head against the DDS / ADA screening scores and the 2019 model.
  • Outputs — two papers (risk flagging; calibration + fairness audit), a chairside score specification, public code repository.

Research questions — Tier 1

  1. RQ1. Can oral-exam-only features (pocket depths, missing teeth, bleeding) flag undiagnosed diabetes and cardiovascular risk in adults?
  2. RQ2. Are such models calibrated (probabilities that mean what they say), temporally stable (validated on a later survey cycle), and demographically fair (subgroup gaps named, not hidden)?
  3. RQ3. Can SHAP-derived explanatory factors be distilled into a chairside risk score — and does it outperform the published DDS / ADA screening scores?

4 · Tier 2 — AI-minimal salivary biomarker panel (flagship)

Concept. Salivary microRNAs detect oral cancer (confirmed by UCLA dental researchers), and an AI classifier exists on the salivary metatranscriptome — but it is a black-box, whole-transcriptome model on a small sample. We build the deployment step: a minimal, survival-linked, tissue-grounded panel, cross-validated from tumor tissue to saliva.

PHASE 1 · PUBLIC · CO-PI

XAI feature selection

TCGA-HNSC: elastic-net + gradient boosting, SHAP stability selection → candidate panel.

→
PHASE 2 · PUBLIC · CO-PI

Salivary detectability screen

GEO saliva datasets: are the candidates measurable and differential?

PHASE 3 · CLINICAL · PI CO-PI

Clinical validation

Ethics → anonymized saliva → RNA extraction → qPCR → blinded classification.

GO / NO-GO — RISK BOUNDED BY DESIGN

Phase 2 costs 2–3 weeks and zero lab budget. Worst case — "no salivary transfer" — is known in month 3, before any sample or ethics investment; that negative result is itself grant-worthy de-risking data. Tissue biology and salivary detectability must BOTH agree before a gene enters the panel. Pivot options: blood-based detection; panel-as-report. A NO-GO retires the saliva-panel approach — not the program: Tier 1 continues untouched, and the de-risking result is publishable.

  • Outputs — two papers (in-silico panel + detectability; clinical validation), an assay-ready panel specification, grant-ready preliminary data.
  • Benchmark — head-to-head on identical data: minimal panel vs whole-transcriptome baseline, and vs the published four-miRNA panel (Ciringione 2025, AUC 0.959).

Research questions — Tier 2

  1. RQ4. Can XAI-driven feature selection on TCGA-HNSC produce a minimal candidate panel that is survival-linked and biologically interpretable?
  2. RQ5. Which panel candidates are detectable in saliva — measurable and differentially expressed in public GEO saliva datasets?
  3. RQ6. Does the final panel discriminate OSCC from controls in the collaborator's clinical saliva samples — matching or beating the published four-miRNA panel head-to-head?

5 · Bandwidth & commitment

The program is phased and public-data-first: Tier 1 requires only a laptop and open datasets; Tier 2's laboratory demands activate only after the month-3 GO/NO-GO gate. Nothing in this proposal requires laboratory expenditure before month 3 — and the first joint manuscript lands within six months regardless of the gate's outcome.
