PROPOSAL OAI-2026 · RESEARCH PROGRAMSTATUS: DRAFT — FOR DISCUSSIONDATE: SEPTEMBER 2026

AI-Driven Oral Health: a two-tier research program — oral–systemic risk prediction · a salivary gene panel for cancer screening

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR (CLINICAL & LAB LEAD)Dr. Shalini Kapoor — Research Dean. Research direction, ethics, cohort & wet-lab validation, corresponding author.
CO-PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR (COMPUTATIONAL LEAD)Kamal Preet Singh — AI/ML Engineer & Data Analyst. Data pipelines, modeling, validation, first-author manuscripts.

Program summary. A direct outcome of the Strategic Research Leadership Meeting's call — "connect ideas, create collaborations." Two projects on one shared computational framework: Tier 1 — reverse oral–systemic risk prediction on NHANES (fast joint paper, public data only); Tier 2 — an AI-minimal salivary biomarker panel for oral cancer screening (TCGA → saliva → clinical validation in the PI's lab). Tier 1 builds the publication record while Tier 2 ramps; both run the same rigor spine: temporal validation, calibration, fairness audit, XAI.

The gap

Dental AI publications are growing rapidly, but 2025 reviews report four recurring gaps [11, 12, 13, 14]:

GAP 01Models are rarely calibrated
GAP 02External / temporal validation is missing
GAP 03Fairness audits are rare
GAP 04Clinical translation is almost nonexistent

The two research ideas below close these four gaps — with first results on public datasets, before any laboratory experiment.

Project 1 — Reverse oral–systemic risk prediction

Make oral data carry clinical signal — earlier than today's tools allow.

What prior work already showed.

  • 2019 NHANES model [4] — predicts periodontitis from systemic factors.
  • Lalla 2011 [1] — unrecognized diabetes can be identified in a dental setting.
  • JADA 2014 [2] — dentists can screen chairside with simple criteria.
  • DDS score [3] — a national-data risk score exists on NHANES 2009–2014.

Our approach. Routine dental-exam features flag undiagnosed diabetes and cardiovascular risk.

A triage flag — says "get a blood test", not "has diabetes".

Data · NHANES (US CDC) — free, public CSVs. ≈5,000–10,000 participants per 2-year cycle — each with BOTH a dental exam and blood results.

  • Subgroup fairness audit — gender · income · ethnicity
  • Temporal validation — train on 2009–14 data, test on 2015–18 data
  • Probability calibration — probabilities that mean what they say
  • SHAP — to produce a calibrated chairside score

Baselines: the DDS score (NHANES 2009–14) [3] and the 2019 model [4].

Research questions — Project 1

  1. RQ1. Can oral-exam-only features (pocket depths, missing teeth, bleeding) flag undiagnosed diabetes and cardiovascular risk in adults?
  2. RQ2. Are such models calibrated, temporally stable, and demographically fair?
  3. RQ3. Can SHAP-derived explanatory factors be distilled into a chairside risk score — and does it outperform the published screening scores [2, 3]?

Expected outcomes — Project 1

PAPER 1 · RISK FLAGGING"Undiagnosed diabetes and cardiovascular risk flagged by routine dental-examination data: a temporally validated model on NHANES 2009–2018." — covers: data pipeline, modeling, temporal validation.
PAPER 2 · CALIBRATION + FAIRNESS"Calibrated and demographically fair chairside screening: a SHAP-distilled oral risk score benchmarked against published criteria." — covers: calibration, fairness audit, chairside score, head-to-head benchmarks [2, 3, 4].

Project 2 — AI-minimal salivary biomarker panel for non-invasive oral cancer screening

The flagship — closest to the PI's biomarker work.

What prior work already showed.

  • UCLA [6] — salivary microRNAs can detect oral cancer.
  • npj 2021 [7] — AI can classify disease from the full salivary transcriptome.
  • Ciringione 2025 [10] — a 4-miRNA panel can reach AUC 0.959.

Our approach. Build a minimal, cheap-to-assay, tissue-derived, survival-linked panel — then cross-validate it from tumor tissue to saliva before any laboratory spend.

Clinical validation

WORST-CASE SCENARIO — BOUNDED BY DESIGN

"No salivary transfer" is known by month 3 — before any sample or ethics investment. Tissue biology and salivary detectability must BOTH agree before a gene enters the panel. On failure: the saliva-panel approach retires — and the de-risking result is publishable. Phase 2 costs 2–3 weeks and zero lab budget.

Baselines: the UCLA miRNA confirmation [6], the npj 2021 classifier [7], and the published four-miRNA panel [10].

Research questions — Project 2

  1. RQ4. Can XAI-driven feature selection on TCGA-HNSC produce a minimal candidate panel that is survival-linked and biologically interpretable?
  2. RQ5. Which panel candidates are detectable in saliva — measurable and differentially expressed in public GEO saliva datasets?
  3. RQ6. Does the final panel discriminate OSCC from controls in clinical saliva samples — matching or beating the published four-miRNA panel [10]?

Expected outcomes — Project 2

PAPER 3 · DISCOVERY + SCREEN"A tumor-grounded, survival-linked minimal salivary gene panel for oral cancer screening: XAI discovery and public-data detectability." — covers: TCGA selection, GEO screen, the GO/NO-GO gate.
PAPER 4 · CLINICAL VALIDATION"Clinical validation of a minimal salivary panel for oral cancer detection: a blinded case–control qPCR study." — covers: cohort, assay, blinded classification, head-to-head benchmark [10].

Phased & public-data-first

The program is phased and public-data-first. Tier 1 asks for open datasets and the PI's clinical validation — nothing else. Tier 2's laboratory demands activate only after month 3. Nothing in this proposal requires laboratory expenditure before month 3 — and the first joint manuscript lands within six months regardless of the gate's outcome.

Risks & mitigation

No candidate is salivary-detectableKnown by month 3 — before any sample or ethics investment. Pivots: blood-based detection, panel-as-report. The negative result de-risks the grant.
Modest predictive signal (Tier 1)The contribution is the rigor package (calibration + fairness + translation) plus head-to-head benchmarks — not headline accuracy.
Variable drift across NHANES cyclesHarmonization phase first; documented mapping tables in the code repository.
Small clinical cohort / qPCR batch effectsPower calculation and pre-registered analysis plan before collection; normalization protocol fixed with the PI.
Bandwidth across parallel projectsPhased program design; separate data tracks; gate-bounded laboratory spend.
