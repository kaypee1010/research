How a salivary gene panel is selected — a demo on a miniature dataset

Prepared by Kamal Preet Singh · September 2026 · Companion documents: research-program-proposal.html · tissue-saliva-bridge-infographic.html

How to read this. The numbers below are illustrative — a demo with 4 genes and 8 patients, built to make the method visible in two minutes. The logic, filters and sources are real: the actual run starts from ~500 patients (TCGA-HNSC) and 40–60 candidate genes, and the published studies cited in section 3 are what this demo is calibrated against.

2 · What has already been done — and the one thing all of it shares

  • Li et al. 2004, Clin Cancer Res (Johns Hopkins) — measured RNA in the saliva of 32 oral-cancer patients + 32 controls; found 1,679 differently-expressed genes; validated a 7-mRNA panel (IL8, IL1B, DUSP1, OAZ1, S100P, SAT, HA3) by qPCR — 91% sensitivity, 91% specificity. Proof that tumor-relevant gene traces reach saliva and carry diagnostic signal. pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15623624
  • npj classifier 2021 — AI on the full salivary transcriptome. Proof that AI can read saliva; but a black box over thousands of targets on a small sample — not a deployable test. nature.com/articles/s41525-021-00257-x
  • Ciringione et al. 2025, IJMS — a 4-miRNA panel (miR-21/31/146a/424), AUC 0.959, n = 60, qRT-PCR. Proof that a minimal panel suffices. The 4 miRNAs were chosen from the 60 saliva measurements themselves. mdpi.com/1422-0067/26/23/11373

The shared pattern: every one of these selected markers from Table 2 alone — the saliva measurements of a small, single-center cohort. None opened Table 1. None asked whether the chosen marker contributes to the tumor, or whether its level tracks survival. That is the gap this program fills.

3 · Our approach — every gene must survive three filters

Filter 1 — Contribution (Table 1). Is the gene actually different in tumor vs normal tissue? A gene can be present in the tumor without driving it — most altered genes are bystanders.

GeneTumor avgNormal avgFold-changeVerdict
GENE_A9.51.17.5×PASS

GENE_C4.24.01.0×FAIL — present in tumor (4.2) but equally present without cancer (4.0): a bystander
GENE_D6.12.03.0×PASS

Filter 2 — Survival link (Table 1). Among tumor patients, does the gene's level track how long they lived? GENE_A: the three early deaths averaged 9.4; the 28-month survivor had 6.2 — higher level, shorter life. Importance is now proven, not assumed. (In the real run this is XAI/SHAP contribution + survival analysis, not eyeballing.) A, B, D pass.

Filter 3 — Salivary detectability (Table 2 — free, laptop, 2–3 weeks). Do the survivors reach saliva, and at measurable levels?

Result: the panel is GENE_A + GENE_D. Each member carries its full evidence chain — tumor contribution, survival link, salivary detectability. GENE_B is the instructive case: a tumor-side hero that dies at the saliva screen — the kind of dead end that, in the real program, is discovered before any lab spend (the month-3 GO/NO-GO gate). GENE_C shows why "present in the tumor" is not the test — "contributing" is.

4 · What the real run looks like

  1. Discovery (TCGA-HNSC, ~500 patients, public) — elastic net + gradient boosting + SHAP stability over 40–60 candidates; survival analysis. → 10–15 survivors. Co-PI, weeks, ₹0.
  2. Saliva screen (GEO public datasets, downloaded) — measurability + differential expression, cross-cohort design. → 5–10. Co-PI, 2–3 weeks, ₹0. GO/NO-GO gate at month 3.
  3. Clinical validation (PI's lab) — only after the gate: anonymized saliva, RNA extraction, qPCR on the 5–10 targets, blinded classification. Head-to-head vs the published 4-miRNA panel (AUC 0.959) and the 7-mRNA panel (91/91).

5 · Why this is novel

Nothing here denies the prior work — it relies on it. Saliva carrying tumor traces: proven since 2004. AI reading saliva: proven 2021. Minimal panels working: proven 2025. The unclaimed step is the selection: a panel that is survival-grounded (Table 1), XAI-explained, screened free on public saliva data before any spend, and validated clinically after a zero-cost gate. Every published panel is a baseline to benchmark against — none of them is the pipeline.
