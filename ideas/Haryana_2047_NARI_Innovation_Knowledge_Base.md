# 3. Strategic Discovery Principle

The starting question should not be:

> "What startup can we build?"

Instead ask:

> **"Which Haryana problem is important enough for a government
> department to care about, already has a policy/programme response, but
> still has an implementation, targeting, prediction, coordination or
> measurement gap?"**

This produces a more defensible innovation.

### The preferred innovation pattern

**Existing Government Goal** → **Existing Government Intervention** →
**Unresolved Data/Execution Gap** → **Prediction / Optimisation /
Decision Layer** → **Targeted Intervention** → **Measured Outcome**

The most promising concepts therefore should not simply duplicate:

-   a government portal
-   a job portal
-   a skill-training programme
-   a health screening camp
-   a transport application
-   an agriculture advisory app

Instead, they should become the **intelligence/orchestration/outcome
layer** around existing systems.

# 4. Women 2047 --- Problem Discovery

## Core question

### What prevents a qualified Haryana woman from entering or staying in the labour market?

The evidence and policy discussion suggest that literacy alone is not
sufficient to explain the participation gap.

A woman may be educated or trained but still face:

-   Care burden: childcare/eldercare
-   Mobility and commuting constraints
-   Safety concerns
-   Social norms/family approval constraints
-   Skills-to-market mismatch
-   Information and digital-access barriers
-   Career interruptions due to marriage, childbirth or care
    responsibilities
-   Workplace conditions and flexibility
-   Health-related interruptions
-   Lack of post-placement support
-   Difficulty re-entering after a career break

The strategic conclusion is:

> **The problem is not simply "women need jobs." It is a multi-stage
> failure of the pathway from capability to sustainable economic
> participation.**

# 6. Why "AI Skill Matching" Is Not Enough

A simple proposition such as:

> "AI skill matching for Haryana women"

is insufficiently differentiated.

Reasons:

1.  Haryana already has a substantial skilling ecosystem.
2.  Job and placement platforms already exist.
3.  Skill matching addresses only one stage.
4.  It does not explain why a woman fails to join a job.
5.  It does not explain why she leaves.
6.  It does not connect employment with childcare, mobility, safety and
    health.
7.  It does not measure sustained outcomes.

A stronger proposition is:

> **Predict the barrier → recommend the intervention → track whether
> employment is sustained.**

# 7. Evaluation Metric Test

Use the official Seva First evaluation dimensions as a design test.

## 7.1 Solution

The solution must solve a documented problem rather than add another
generic app.

Preferred formulation:

> Predict → Intervene → Measure

## 7.2 Uniqueness

Novelty should come from the **system architecture and outcome loop**,
not from merely adding the word "AI".

## 7.3 Impact

Impact should be measurable.

Examples:

-   women entering employment
-   3/6/12-month retention
-   re-entry after career interruption
-   income improvement
-   progression
-   health-risk follow-up
-   referral completion
-   reduced avoidable work interruption

## 7.4 Scalability

The architecture should support:

**Pilot district → Haryana → other states**

## 7.5 Clarity

A jury member should understand the problem and solution within one
sentence.

## 7.6 Problem Relevance

The concept should map directly to a documented Haryana priority.

## 7.7 Existing Solutions

Existing programmes are not necessarily a weakness.

They can reveal the **missing intelligence layer**.

## 7.8 Value Proposition

Government should be able to answer:

> What is happening?

> Why is it happening?

> What should we do?

> Did it work?

## 7.9 Potential Impact

Prefer population-level outcomes over individual convenience features.

## 7.10 Market Size

Potential customers/users include:

-   state departments
-   district administration
-   employers
-   industrial clusters
-   universities
-   skill institutions
-   healthcare institutions
-   other states

## 7.11 Ease of Scalability

Prefer modular software/data architecture over infrastructure-heavy
models.

# 9. NARI-CONTINUITY AI --- Key Features

## 9.1 Employment Barrier Prediction

Predict whether a woman is likely to face difficulty entering or
retaining employment.

Possible variables:

-   education
-   skills
-   employment history
-   location
-   commute
-   transport availability
-   childcare
-   work timing
-   job characteristics
-   digital access
-   employer flexibility
-   self-reported constraints
-   health-related factors where appropriately governed

## 9.2 AI Job/Opportunity Matching

Match not only on skill but on **workability**.

Example:

``` text
Skill fit       HIGH
Salary fit      HIGH
Distance        MEDIUM
Transport       LOW
Childcare       LOW
Timing          LOW

Primary barrier:
Childcare + work timing
```

## 9.3 Geospatial Accessibility

Analyse:

-   residence
-   employment clusters
-   transport routes
-   commute time
-   childcare locations
-   safety factors
-   working hours

## 9.4 Intervention Recommendation

Instead of simply saying:

> "Here are jobs."

The system says:

> "This woman could realistically take Job X if childcare and transport
> constraints are addressed."

## 9.5 Retention Analytics

Track:

-   30 days
-   3 months
-   6 months
-   12 months

## 9.6 Re-entry Intelligence

Identify women who have left work and estimate:

-   readiness
-   skills gap
-   likely barriers
-   suitable opportunities
-   required intervention

# 15. Why This Concept Is Different

Avoid:

> "AI disease diagnosis."

Use:

> **AI-assisted risk stratification + clinician validation + referral +
> follow-up.**

The objective is not to replace doctors.

The objective is to prevent the workflow from ending at:

**Screened → referred**

Instead:

**Screened → risk identified → clinician validates → referral →
treatment/follow-up → continuity**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 34. "Don't Build Another App" Test

Before approving any future idea, ask:

### Question 1

Does Haryana already have a scheme/programme doing this?

If yes, continue.

### Question 2

What is the unresolved gap?

Possible gaps:

-   prediction
-   targeting
-   coordination
-   measurement
-   optimisation
-   follow-up
-   outcome verification

### Question 3

Does the innovation improve a measurable government outcome?

If not, rethink.

### Question 4

Can a prototype demonstrate the mechanism before the deadline?

If not, reduce scope.

### Question 5

Can the concept scale beyond one district?

If not, examine whether it is a service/project rather than a scalable
startup.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 35. Preferred Prototype Philosophy

For Seva First, the prototype should demonstrate:

## 1. The problem

Use a real Haryana policy problem.

## 2. The data

Show what inputs the system needs.

## 3. The intelligence

Show prediction/recommendation.

## 4. The intervention

Show what a department/user does next.

## 5. The outcome

Show how success is measured.

The jury should be able to understand:

> **Input → AI → Action → Outcome**

# 36. Jury Demonstration Story

Use a human story rather than beginning with technology.

Example:

> "Meet a 28-year-old Haryana woman. She is educated and has accounting
> skills. She wants to work, but has two children, limited transport and

``` text
Traditional system:
Here are 27 jobs.

NARI:
```

Then show:

``` text
Barrier:
Childcare + timing

Nearby creche + compatible shift + transport
```

Then:

``` text
Outcome:
Joined
→ retained 3 months
→ retained 6 months
→ income improved
```

> "But what if the barrier is not access to work, but an unmanaged

Then demonstrate:

``` text
Screen
→ AI risk stratification
→ clinician validation
→ referral
→ follow-up
→ continuity
```

# 39. Data Governance Principles

Particularly important for the Health-to-Work concept.

Use:

-   consent-based data collection
-   data minimisation
-   role-based access
-   de-identification where possible
-   audit trails
-   clinician oversight for medical decisions
-   explainable AI
-   human override
-   no automated adverse employment decisions
-   separation of sensitive health data from unnecessary employer access

The system should **support** decisions, not automatically deny jobs or
benefits.

# 43. Key Takeaways

1.  **Start with a Haryana policy problem, not a technology.**
2.  **Do not duplicate existing government programmes.**
3.  **Build the intelligence layer around them.**
4.  **Use prediction, targeting, optimisation and outcome measurement.**
5.  **Make every intervention measurable.**
6.  **Keep the MVP narrow enough to demonstrate before the deadline.**
7.  **Use SGT's multidisciplinary strengths as a genuine advantage.**
8.  **Keep clinical AI clinician-supervised.**
9.  **Use NARI-Continuity as the employment intelligence module.**
10. **Use NARI Health-to-Work as the preventive health/continuity
    module.**
11. **Treat NARI Voice as an accessibility module, not necessarily a
    separate startup.**
12. **Keep agriculture, governance and urban resilience as alternative
    opportunity tracks.**
13. **The strongest presentation is Input → Intelligence → Intervention
    → Outcome.**
14. **The ultimate policy question is not "How many women did we train?"
    but "How many women entered, stayed and returned to sustainable
    economic activity?"**
