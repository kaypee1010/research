Research Thrust Areas

Kamal Preet Singh · Assistant Professor, CSE, SOET · SGT University

My research applies machine learning to two areas where SGT University has strong in-house capacity: engineering education and healthcare. Both thrusts share one approach: models that report how confident they are and explain their output, so that risk can be identified early and acted on.

1. Confidence-Aware & Explainable Learning Analytics

This thrust builds on SGIP, the Student Growth Intelligence Platform, which I have developed since joining SGT. SGIP records post-lecture quizzes, lab evaluations and competition responses, along with how confident each student felt about each answer. It turns this into a growth profile for each student and a topic-difficulty view for faculty. The student interface and the faculty dashboard are complete, and the SGIP proposal has now been sanctioned by the University. Set-up for the first pilot with live classes is under way.

The research question is whether combining answer accuracy, stated confidence and response time can identify a struggling student early enough to intervene, and whether the model can explain each flag clearly enough for faculty to trust it and act on it. Once the pilot is running, it will provide the main data for Year-1 publications in this thrust. Alongside this, my earlier deep-learning work on rumour and misinformation detection on Twitter (over 100,000 tweets, including COVID-19 data) shares the same focus on explainability and can be written up without new data collection.

2. AI/ML for Clinical & Healthcare Data Analytics

Before moving into academia full-time, I spent about two years in data roles in Australia, including a year as a data consultant at HCF Insurance, a health insurer in Sydney, where I built Snowflake ETL pipelines over more than 10 million records. With SGT’s own medical college and hospital, the natural next step is to apply the confidence-aware, explainable approach from Thrust 1 to clinical and hospital-operations data: early risk flagging from patient records, readmission or no-show prediction, and triage support. These models would serve only as decision support for clinicians, and all work would run under IEC and data-governance approval.

This thrust depends on collaboration with faculty at SGT Medical College & Hospital and on ethics clearance. Year 1 is therefore planned as a scoping year: building the collaboration, co-drafting a funding proposal, and starting pilot studies once data access and ethics approval are in place.

Priorities for Year 1

  • Thrust 1 is the main focus in Year 1, now that SGIP has been sanctioned and its pilot is being set up.
  • Thrust 2 is paced across the year, as it depends on ethics approval and data access from SGT Medical College & Hospital.
  • The misinformation work sits within Thrust 1 as an additional source of publications. It is not a separate thrust.
