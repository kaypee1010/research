Research Thrust Areas

Kamal Preet Singh · Assistant Professor, CSE, SOET · SGT University

My proposed research agenda for the RTF role is centred on applied machine learning in two settings where SGT has clear institutional strength: engineering education and healthcare. The common thread across both is the use of explainable, uncertainty-aware models to identify risk early enough to support timely intervention. I have intentionally kept the agenda focused on two connected thrust areas that can be pursued realistically alongside a 12-hour teaching load.

1. Confidence-Aware & Explainable Learning Analytics

The first thrust grows directly out of SGIP, the Student Growth Intelligence Platform that I have been developing. The platform is designed to combine post-lecture quizzes, lab evaluations, and student-reported confidence into a more informative picture of learning progress. In principle, this allows the system to do two useful things: identify students who may be falling behind before that pattern is visible in final grades, and help faculty see which topics are consistently producing low-confidence responses across a class. At present, SGIP is at the MVP stage. The student interface and faculty/admin dashboard are in place, but a live institutional pilot still depends on formal sanction and budget support, especially for the recommendation layer built around LLM calls.

Within the RTF framework, the research question I want to pursue is straightforward: does the combination of answer accuracy, stated confidence, and response behaviour improve early identification of academic risk, and can that prediction be explained in a way that faculty will actually trust and use? If the SGIP pilot is approved, this thrust should become the main source of Year-1 publications. If approval takes longer, I can still move immediately on my earlier deep-learning work on rumour and misinformation detection on Twitter, based on datasets of 100K+ tweets including COVID-19-era data. That work uses existing data, shares the same interest in explainability, and gives me a practical publication pipeline while the SGIP pilot is being formalised.

2. AI/ML for Clinical & Healthcare Data Analytics

The second thrust follows from both my prior industry experience and SGT’s institutional context. Before returning fully to academia, I worked as a data engineer and analyst in Australia, including at HCF Insurance in Sydney, where I built Snowflake ETL pipelines over large healthcare datasets. Given that background, and given that SGT includes a medical college and hospital, a natural next step is to develop machine-learning methods for clinical and healthcare data analytics: early risk flagging from patient records, readmission or no-show prediction, and related decision-support problems. The emphasis here would remain on interpretable support systems, not automated clinical decision-making, and any such work would proceed only within proper ethics and data-governance approval.

Because this thrust depends on collaboration, data access, and IEC approval, I see Year 1 mainly as a scoping and relationship-building phase. The immediate goal is to identify the right clinical collaborators, define one or two tractable problems, and co-develop a funding proposal strong enough to support pilot work later. I would rather state that dependency clearly now than over-promise a timeline that depends on approvals outside my control.

How This Fits a 12-Hour Teaching Load

  • Thrust 1 is the nearer-term priority because it is closer to my current teaching and development work, though its full research output still depends on the SGIP pilot being sanctioned.
  • The misinformation/NLP work remains an immediate fallback within Thrust 1 because it can be written up without waiting on new approvals or new data collection.
  • Thrust 2 is deliberately paced more cautiously across Year 1, since progress there depends on collaborators, ethics clearance, and data access.
  • I have not proposed a third, unrelated thrust. The intention is to build depth in two connected areas rather than spread effort too thinly.

I would welcome the Committee’s guidance on sequencing these priorities, and particularly on possible introductions within SGT Medical College that could help this agenda develop well.
