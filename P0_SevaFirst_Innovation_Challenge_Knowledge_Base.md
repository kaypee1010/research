# SEVA FIRST INNOVATION CHALLENGE — AGENT KNOWLEDGE BASE

Official-source briefing extracted from the Startup Haryana Seva First Innovation Challenge website

**Primary source:** https://sevafirstinnovationchallenge.startupharyana.gov.in/

**Source status:** Source accessed/retrieved September 2026


## 1. PURPOSE OF THIS KNOWLEDGE BASE

This document is designed to be attached to an AI/agent prompt so the agent can understand the Seva First Innovation Challenge without first needing to extract the basic challenge information from the website.

It captures the challenge information explicitly presented on the official Startup Haryana portal. It should be treated as a source briefing, not as a substitute for checking the live portal for changes, deadlines, application fields, or programme terms.


## 2. OFFICIAL IDENTITY

Name: Seva First Innovation Challenge.

Host/initiative context: The portal identifies the challenge as an initiative of STARTUP HARYANA.

Broader context: The challenge is presented as part of the 'Sewa Sankalp Abhiyan' and its Innovation Module.

The stated purpose is to harness the innovative potential of citizens and promote creativity, problem-solving, and citizen participation.


## 3. VISION

The stated vision is to foster a deep-rooted culture of innovation and empower talented individuals and enterprises to contribute towards Haryana's transformation.

The portal emphasizes solutions that are impactful, sustainable, and scalable.

The solutions are intended to address urgent public and industrial challenges.


## 4. MISSION

The stated mission is to provide a transparent and accessible digital platform connecting innovators with state departments, academia, and industry leaders.

The platform is positioned as supporting entrepreneurship and live demonstration opportunities.

The portal states that participation is provided with zero fees.


## 5. WHO CAN PARTICIPATE

Student — college, polytechnic and university students with fresh technological or creative ideas.

Startup — early-stage and scaling ventures developing disruptive products, technologies, or business models.

Professional — domain experts, engineers, technology leaders, and corporate practitioners addressing practical industry bottlenecks.

Innovator — grassroots inventors, independent problem-solvers, and creative thinkers, including those with working prototypes.

Researcher — academicians, scientists, faculty, and research scholars translating research/lab discovery into public impact.

Citizen — active citizens, community leaders, and local residents interested in civic improvement.

Others — the portal specifically includes enthusiasts, NGOs, and self-help groups.


## 6. ELIGIBILITY / PARTICIPATION FACTS

The challenge is described as open to participants from diverse backgrounds across Haryana.

Haryana-based startups, MSMEs, professionals, and innovators are encouraged to participate through support/facilitation associated with the Industries & Commerce Department and associated agencies.

Individuals can apply without having a registered company; individual and grassroots innovators are explicitly eligible.

Registration and participation are stated to be free.

Multiple innovations may be submitted, subject to the challenge terms; each innovation should be submitted through a separate application.


## 7. APPLICATION WORKFLOW

Step 1 — Registration and Login: enter a mobile number, verify through OTP, and generate a unique Participant Registration ID.

Step 2 — Submit Idea Proposal: select a challenge sector, outline the problem and solution, and upload a project brief or presentation.

Step 3 — Jury Evaluation: domain experts, mentors, and departmental officers review submissions for innovation, feasibility, and impact.

Step 4 — Rewards and Recognition: shortlisted participants/startups receive an opportunity to present their ideas to the State-level jury, followed by rewards and recognition for winners.


## 8. WHAT CAN BE SUBMITTED

The portal describes the challenge as identifying, promoting, and supporting innovative ideas, products, technologies, and solutions.

Prototype-stage and market-ready innovations can be considered, provided they demonstrate originality and potential impact.

Supporting documents, images, videos, or presentations may be uploaded where applicable.

## 10. OFFICIAL EVALUATION DIMENSIONS

The portal states that innovations may be evaluated on the following dimensions:

- Proposed Solution

- Uniqueness

- Impact Created

- Scalability

- Clarity and Completeness of Idea

- Problem Relevance

- Whether the idea already exists in the market

- Value Proposition

- Potential Impact

- Market Size

- Whether the idea is scalable

- Ease of scalability


## 11. INTERPRETATION OF THE EVALUATION LENS

The following is an analytical interpretation for agent use, not an official scoring rubric.

PROBLEM RELEVANCE: The problem should be clearly defined and sufficiently important to justify intervention.

PROPOSED SOLUTION: The mechanism connecting the identified problem to the intended outcome should be concrete and understandable.

UNIQUENESS: The agent should establish what is genuinely differentiated rather than assuming novelty because technology is used.

EXISTING MARKET: The agent should investigate existing products, startups, government programmes, portals, schemes, research projects and analogous solutions.

VALUE PROPOSITION: The proposal should explain who receives value, what changes, and why the proposed approach is preferable/different.

IMPACT: The proposal should identify measurable outcomes rather than relying on broad claims.

SCALABILITY: The agent should explain whether the innovation can move beyond a small pilot.

EASE OF SCALABILITY: The agent should examine institutional, technical, financial, operational and adoption barriers to expansion.

CLARITY & COMPLETENESS: The submission should allow an evaluator to understand the problem, solution, differentiation, impact and scale pathway without filling major gaps themselves.


## 12. AWARDS AND RECOGNITION

The portal states that one top performer from each district will receive a cash prize of ₹1,00,000.

The portal states that the three best performers across all applicants will receive ₹5,00,000 each.

The portal describes recognition and opportunities to present selected ideas to a State-level jury.


## 13. EVALUATION / SELECTION PROCESS

The portal indicates that evaluation may include application screening, preliminary assessment, presentation and jury evaluation of selected applicants, and final selection/recognition.

The stated review participants include domain experts, mentors, and departmental officers.

The portal specifically associates jury review with innovation, feasibility, and impact.


## 14. STRATEGIC POSITIONING OF THE CHALLENGE

The portal positions Seva First as an open innovation bridge connecting students, researchers, citizens and innovators with administrative departments across Haryana.

It is intended to facilitate co-creation of actionable governance and societal solutions.

The challenge is therefore not restricted to conventional startup products; it explicitly accommodates public, civic, industrial, research and grassroots innovation.


## 15. IMPLICATIONS FOR AN AI INNOVATION AGENT

1. Do not optimize only for technical sophistication. The official portal explicitly emphasizes problem relevance, impact, feasibility, uniqueness and scalability.

2. Novelty must be investigated. The portal explicitly asks whether an idea already exists in the market.

3. A clear value proposition is required.

4. Market size matters, even for solutions with public-sector or social objectives.

5. Scalability and ease of scalability are separately identified; the agent should examine both the theoretical ability to scale and the practical friction of scaling.

6. Prototype-stage concepts are eligible, so a fully commercialized product is not required.

7. Market-ready innovations are also eligible.

8. Public-sector and civic impact should be considered because the platform explicitly connects innovators with state departments.

9. The agent should consider both public and industrial challenges because the stated vision explicitly references both.

10. The submission should be concise and sufficiently complete because the workflow asks applicants to outline the problem and solution and upload a project brief or presentation.


## 16. ANTI-DUPLICATION CHECK — MANDATORY

Because the official criteria explicitly ask whether the idea already exists in the market, every serious candidate should undergo a duplication check before being treated as challenge-ready.

The agent should search for:

- existing government schemes and portals;

- Haryana government initiatives;

- Indian government platforms;

- startups and commercial products;

- academic/research projects;

- patents and technology disclosures where relevant;

- analogous international solutions;

- open-source projects where relevant.

The output should distinguish exact duplication, functional similarity, adjacent competition, and genuine white space.


## 17. SEVAFIRST-READY IDEA TEST

An idea should be treated as stronger when it can clearly answer:

- What urgent problem does it solve?

- Who experiences that problem?

- Why is the problem relevant to Haryana/public or industrial outcomes?

- What exactly is the proposed solution?

- What is genuinely unique?

- Does something similar already exist?

- What is the value proposition?

- What measurable impact can result?

- What is the addressable market or beneficiary base?

- How does it scale?

- What makes scaling practically easy or difficult?

- Can the idea demonstrate feasibility?

## 19. RECOMMENDED AGENT OPERATING RULES BASED ON THIS SOURCE

RULE A — Treat the official criteria as the primary SevaFirst evaluator lens.

RULE B — Do not invent weights for the official criteria.

RULE C — Treat uniqueness as an evidence question, not a narrative claim.

RULE D — Treat scalability as both an outcome and an implementation problem.

RULE E — Test market existence explicitly.

RULE F — Quantify impact wherever credible evidence permits.

RULE G — Distinguish prototype readiness from market readiness.

RULE H — Explain value proposition separately from technology.

RULE I — Investigate public-sector implementation because the platform explicitly connects innovators with state departments.

RULE J — Do not assume that winning the challenge automatically creates funding or government adoption.
